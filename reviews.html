Reviews

My rubric:
★★★★★: Undeniably special. When these ones hit you, you'll know.
★★★★☆: Warmly recommended.
★★★☆☆: Average-to-good. Holds water.
★★☆☆☆: Obvious flaws, or doesn't quite make the grade.
★☆☆☆☆: Very flawed.
☆☆☆☆☆: Physically unpleasant to experience. When these ones hit you, you'll also know.

A Doggone Christmas

★★☆☆☆ | A telepathic dog battles the CIA.

Awful, delightful and hilarious, albeit unintentionally so. The premade food, the cheap costumes, and the awful acting combine to more than the sum of its parts. It's bad, but I cannot in good conscience give a movie that got this many snorts out of me a single star. Merry Christmas to all.

A Fistful of Dollars

★★★⯪☆ | A stranger gets in a lot of fights in a dangerous town.

Gritty, sweaty, hard-livin' Spaghetti Western stuff. Nice that the hero uses cunning as well as gunplay.

Abbott Elementary

★★★★⯪ | Teachers try their best in a habitually underfunded Philadelphia school.

Consistently funny, great cast, doesn't talk down to the audience, solid messaging. TV needs more of this right now.

American Harmony

★★★☆☆ | Barbershop quartets go for the gold.

Strong "homemade church congregation film" vibes, but frames the journey surprisingly well. Pulls on the same strings as a good sports documentary.

Appleseed (1988)

★★★☆☆ | Futuristic anime police battle terrorists.

Well-drawn, but a bit holey with its story. Focused on the idea of "utopia," but doesn't take the time to slow down for a minute and explore the concept. Simultaneously generous and a bit thin.

Batman: Arkham City

★★★★⯪ | Arkham Asylum, but open-world.

Damn, this is how you make a superhero game! Intelligently made, killer combat system, and tons of fanservice in regards to lore. Surprise favorite performance: Maurice Lamarche as Calendar Man. Pretty impressive how much fun they made crisscrossing the city via gliding and grapnelling. Always a bunch of cute happenings being generated as the player travels around. A very 'playable' game, in the most literal sense. If the visuals had a bit more of a more daring flair, it'd get a perfect score from me.

The Blair Witch Project

★★★★★ | A found-footage spiral in the forests of Maryland.

Probably my very favorite horror movie, and a masterclass of doing a lot with a little.

Broad City

★★★★☆ | Best-friendship by way of enabling disaster.

A pretty great premise - messy, funny, authentically poor friends leave a trail of chaos behind them in a city so large, consequences only sometimes follow. More hits than misses.

The Cabin in the Woods

★★★⯪☆ | Five teens get wrapped up in a global horror conspiracy.

Tropey and entertaining. Very aware film that was interested in playing around with meta-knowledge for a neat gimmick. Switches to a very Sam Raimi-sort of feeling for the finale, but that's never a bad thing.

Carry-On

★★★⯪☆ | A TSA worker gets caught in the middle of an attack in an airport.

Taron Edgerton flexes to elevate a decent script to a good performance. Strains credulity in quite a few places, but provides some solid thrills.

Castle in the Sky

★★★★⯪ | A princess and a coal miner discover there's more (and less) to the world than they know.

Absolutely wild Ghibli came out of the gate with this one. The brief usage of high camera angles to show the figures as petty and antlike in their greed and wrath has always stuck with me. I want to give this five stars, but it falls just a hair short. (Can you believe that train chase in the canyon?)

Chainsaw Man - The Movie: Reze Arc

★★★★⯪ | Doomed, doomed millenial love.

Stunning animation, but this movie actually tends to wow the most in the small, emotional moments, where Fujimoto can really dig his heels into the "millenial melancholy" that CM excels in. A rare movie that I completely feel the ticket price was worth.

Chris Porter: Ugly and Angry

★★⯪☆☆ | Standup comedy about the frustrations of everyday life.

Not quite my brand of comedy, but it did get a few chuckles out of me. There's a lot to be angry about with our modern world, I guess. Funny, relatable stuff intended for a different sense of humor.

Cromwell

★★★☆☆ | The English Civil Wars by way of 70's epic period dramas.

There's something about this movie that feels a bit hollow and unbalanced. I think it's the grandiosity of the big battle scene, compared to the numerous debates shot with static cameras, figures looming over tables. Alec Guinness and Richard Harris are both excellent and get a few solid moments apiece, but other than that, mostly a chest-thumping affair of fiery conviction and such.

Cruel Intentions

★★⯪☆☆ | Two stepsiblings engage in an incestuous sex bet.

A weirdly soap-operaish film. It's halfway between teen drama and messy sex drama. Intentionally trashy, but it does capture a specific moment in time.

The Devil's Advocate

★★★⯪☆ | A lawyer discovers his client is Satan.

I think this film is almost excellent. A little uneven, maybe a half-hour too long, and slides between camp and horror pretty freely. Keanu Reeves doesn't quite sell me all the way, but Al Pacino puts the movie on his back confidently. Very much of its time.

Dicey Dungeons

★★★☆☆ | Dungeon crawling in a RNG duel against Lady Luck.

Solid little dungeon crawler, but bafflingly, it gets the dice wrong!! Dice don't roll, they slide onscreen with no animation, having already landed on a number. No clacking noises, no particles... what's going on here? No button to roll the dice??? Dang. Music is utterly outstanding, though.

The Epic of Gilgamesh (David Ferry translation)

★★★★☆ | The world's most powerful king learns his place in the universe.

Surprising to see how complete it is as a narrative. I don't get to read stories in verse often. A compelling meditation on change and death. I'm glad this is the oldest recorded story.

Escape from New York

★★★☆☆ | A retired military criminal is press-ganged into rescuing the President.

It's got heart, but can't quite outrun its limits. Portraying an entire city under siege and ruin is ambitious, and the movie doesn't quite have the resources to establish space and place particularly well.

Final Fantasy VIII

★★★⯪☆ | Child soldiers navigate war, adolescence, and the supernatural.

A lot to love and a lot to be annoyed by, but that's to it's credit - this game takes big swings. Probably a few too many ideas for its own good, but I give a lot of credit to Square for not playing it safe after FFVII. Had I grasped the GF system better, this would probably have another half a star.

Gods and Generals

★⯪☆☆☆ | The first half of the American Civil War from the perspective of Stonewall Jackson.

Too generous to Lost Cause narratives than is comfortable, too eager to engage in revisionism than I'd like. The scenes of Confederate soldiers and slaves praying together to find a way to free them someday are absurd and unpleasant.

Goodfellas

★★★★☆ | An exploration of the darkness and splendor of organized crime.

This film makes gangster life so pretty, yet so ugly. The violence happens in sudden bursts. The sense of paranoia as the second act falls apart is palpable.

Gun Godz

★★★☆☆ | An FPS that takes place on Venus.

Simple and straightforward, if a bit stiff. Reminds me of stuff like Rise of the Triad.

The Haunted Mansion (2003)

★★★☆☆ | A realtor and his family discover the house he has been hired to sell is haunted.

Terrence Stamp steals the show. Mostly intended to be an Eddie Murphy vehicle, but is a genuinely fun time in spite of that. More fun than I remembered (but as a Parks diehard, I can't say the definitive HM movie really exists yet).

House II: The Second Story

★☆☆☆☆ | Another mansion with another supernatural curse. I think?

Utterly baffling. This movie mentions and abandons ideas and tumbles down the metaphorical hill, becoming a horror film, a family adventure, and finally a western. Must be seen to be believed. John Ratzenberger steals the show.

Hundreds of Beavers

★★★☆☆ | An applejack salesman becomes a fur trapper.

The focus on looking backward holds this movie back in a tiny way I can't place. Unhinged, gory, and loaded with physical comedy. Probably could be a bit shorter than it ended up being. Quite a few video game sensibilities!

I Am Big Bird

★★★⯪☆ | Sesame Street Muppeteer Caroll Spinney tells his story.

A documentary of humility and bittersweetness (as stories that honestly talk about life often are). A lovely exploration of the connection between artist and character.

Jacob's Ladder

★★★★★ | A Vietnam veteran struggles between the boundary of reality and falsehood.

This is the kind of film where you can tell how seminal it is just by watching. Sympathetic, pained, and haunting. A fantastic example of how to breed poignancy with context in film.

John Wick

★★★★☆ | The world's most dangerous hitman goes on a dog-revenge spree against the bratva.

A very lean, single-minded revenge movie. Being made by stunt professionals shows - all the action is ruthless and visceral. Slightly corny worldbuilding (I'm one to talk, really) smoothed over by a cast with tons of charisma and conviction.

K-Pop Demon Hunters

★★★⯪☆ | Enemies-to-lovers with a lot of trendy musical numbers.

A fun time, but I wouldn't call it well-written. Very loose worldbuilding with a tendency to leave its own ideas hanging a bit limply. Second half plays tag with itself a bit and loses a lot of the joy. Quite good music, fun gags, and man, that animation. Shines when it's not afraid to get "goobery" with its main cast.

Kiki's Delivery Service

★★★★★ | A young witch tries to figure out how to live on her own.

Fantastic. It's extremely rare to see a studio of middle-aged men understand young women like this script does, and it speaks to Ghibli's commitment to nuance.

Kirby Star Allies

★★★⯪☆ | Kirby battles a dark priest through the power of (literal) friendship.

Solidly built, but didn't move me much emotionally. The Friend Heart system mostly remixes ideas from other Kirby games (helpers from Super Star, Four-Kirby from Amazing Mirror, etc.). The end result is fun gameplay, but I feel as though I've seen it before.

Some minor feel issues - Friend Abilities should be a button press rather than an analog stick tilt. Friend Hearts should travel at a more satisfying speed. Roster of characters and lore is quite good.

Klaus

★★★★⯪ | A postmaster learns the meaning of life.

Well now! A Christmas movie that doesn't pull its punches! Delightful, sincere, and engaging.

I Love My Computer - Ninajirachi

★★★★☆ | Grimy, jittery, enthusiastic electropop about early internet tech culture.

A great opportunity to stop and reflect on the Frutiger Aero 2000's and its distance to the modern internet. A great love letter to what was. Fantastic flow between songs. Kickass record.

Independence Day

★★⯪☆☆ | America saves the day after aliens attack.

It's very good at being a popcorn movie. The miniature work is absolutely fantastic, and the story holds water about 60% of the time. It's absurd, but it cannot be argued that Roland Emmerich understood the assignment.

Lawrence of Arabia

★★★★★ | Identity, allegiance, and the darkness of man in the Arab campaign of World War 1.

Utterly huge, even on a small screen. The biggest compliment I can give is that it makes the desert as hypnotic and humbling for the audience as it does for the cast.

Mario Pinball Land

★★⯪☆☆ | Mario uses a device that crushes his body into a ball to rescue Princess Peach...?

The lack of interest this game shows in explaining or contextualizing its pinball elements is disappointing to me. It's not just about worldbuilding, but thoughtful thinking about how pinball-people would interact with blocks or coins. Wouldn't they be differently shaped for their lack of hands? Nintendo would have engaged in some of that design spirit, but I guess that wasn't important to Fuse Games.

The Martian

★★★★⯪ | Grimy, jittery, enthusiastic electropop about early internet tech culture.

For those who love seeing competent, quick-thinking people find a way through imposing doom, this is a movie for you. A very problem-solving kind of film. Feels similar to the emotional curve of a heist movie.

Monster House

★★★☆☆ | Neighborhood kids venture inside a living haunted house.

Pretty harsh / intense movie by kids' standards. Not quite to my personal preference, but no major flaws to speak of.

The Mystic Knights of Tir Na Nog

★★☆☆☆ | Action-adventure by way of Irish mythological tokosatsu.

A rather disposable, but funny blend of Saban cheese and nondescript high fantasy. Shoestring-budget television, but unintentionally funny until it starts to wear out its welcome. You can feel the showrunners praying for a successful action figure run as you watch.

The Neverending Story

★★★⯪☆ | A boy learns to accept the loss of his mother.

A few obvious bits could be improved, but the movie puts you in a forgiving mood. Uneasy, grotesque, but also vivid and creative. The meta elements encourage the audience to come away with something after they're done. I deeply respect how must trust this puts in children.

The Night Before

★☆☆☆☆ | Three friends search for a rumored Christmas party.

Weak writing. Characters don't behave logically and the supporting cast is way too forgiving of everything.

The Night is Short, Walk On Girl

★★★★★ | A college sophomore explores the nightlife of Tokyo.

Put a smile on my face. I love stories that suggest that the world has a whimsical or magical side, unnoticed or just out of reach.

Obsession

★★★★⯪ | A man makes a bad wish.

Impressively terrifying. Had me stressed out the entire watch. Inde Navarrette puts on an utterly monstrous performance and crushes the film.

The Odyssey (2026)

★★★★☆ | Odysseus tries to get home.

Hell of a good adaptation. The story is framed around a poignant idea with a lot of bleak relevance to the present day. The Hades sequence is unforgettable. Extremely loud - my torso was vibrating and I could hear the dialogue through my earplugs loud. John Leguizamo steals his scenes. I'm a little infuriated that Nolan managed to sneak in TWO twists without changing the plot into a 3000 year old story that I already knew. (They're really, really good twists though.)

The Peanut Butter Solution

☆☆☆☆☆ | Ghosts help a boy magically regrow his hair.

I have no idea what to make of this thing. This film has no idea what to do with any of the emotions its story beats generate. Zero consideration for how to steer the audience towards a feeling, or how to move the barometer. Surreal, off, and unpleasant.

The Polar Express (2004)

★★★⯪☆ | A boy is reminded of the power of believing on Christmas Eve.

It's loud, it has a big heart, and to its credit, Zemeckis is still Zemeckis. I just, in my gut, feel as though the best qualities of the film probably came from the book.

Pokémon Concierge

★★★★☆ | A new concierge at a resort learns how to care for Pokémon.

Very gentle and sweet. Feels like taking a breath of clear beachside air. Seeing the Pokémon in fuzzy stop-motion is the real treat, but the characters and story add coziness as well. My god, the Pokémon are so fuzzy...

Pokémon Platinum

★★★★☆ | A trainer befriends Cynthia and saves the world from Team Galactic.

Quite a solid version of Pokémon. Mt. Coronet is probably the best dungeon I've seen in the series: the way it uses indoor and outdoor sections, the fog as you climb higher, the team galactic grunts hiding behind rocks. Very enjoyable and tense. Distortion World is compelling, weird, and definitely my favorite part of the game. I appreciate that the Gym Challenge puzzles were a bit knotty without being torturous. I enjoyed having Cynthia as a travel buddy throughout the game, it made the climax of fighting her at the end all the more special.

Porco Rosso

★★★★★ | A seaplane pilot lives his life under the shadow of Italian fascism.

As with all Ghibli, beautiful in all the ways one can categorize. Dashing, romantic sensibilities with a lot of world-weariness. Cigarettes and coastal wine. My favorite of Ghibli's films.

Pumpkinhead II: Blood Wings

★☆☆☆☆ | Teens resurrect Pumpkinhead.

Miss. Lance Henriksen's abscence is sorely felt, but he couldn't have saved this. Root for the monster.

Puyo Puyo Tetris 2

★★★☆☆ | More Puyo, more Tetris!

About as good as the first game, but mostly because it doesn't introduce anything new, mostly. Story Mode is still very well-acted and charming, with a rather samey presentation. The game slides along until Chapter 4, Stage 3, when the Puyo difficulty suddenly grows muscles and slammed me through a wall.

Revolutionary Girl Utena

★★★★⯪ | Duels, romance, and dreamlike mystery inside a strange school.

Layered, meaningful, and deeply mysterious. A deeply special piece of work that manages to push a cause without coming across as preachy, which barely any works manage to. I could do with less repetition overall, but that's probably both a budgetary concession and a deliberate artistic choice. You will scarcely come across a more intense usage of symbolism than this show.

Ride Your Wave

★★★⯪☆ | A firefighter and a surfer fall in love.

It's disposable, but it's delightful and really excellently made. Gets pretty fantastical, but with a good emotional core at all times. Nice usage of fire and water.

The Ring

★★★★★ | Haunted VHS tapes.

Deeply impressive. The tension keeps the screws in all the way through. The color grading of this film is so insanely heavy - it clings to the audience like a wet blanket. Fantastic example of stretching a PG-13 rating as far as it can go. A cut above in nearly all categories.

The Rocky Horror Picture Show

★★★★☆ | A straight-laced couple discovers it's fun to be naughty.

Fascinating. Punk. Rebellious. Must have been a great refuge for those that needed it in its era. I understand why it endures.

Scooby-Doo on Zombie Island

★★★⯪☆ | "This time, the ghosts are real!"

Much different to watch as an adult than a child. Very well-animated by SD standards (thanks Mook Animation), but the inclusion of certain details and time periods adds a bit of messiness to the ethics of the whole thing.

Shrek

★★★★☆ | An ogre and a donkey attempt to rescue a princess.

The rebellious nature comes across as a bit adolescent, but the message of "inner beauty" still resonates (even though it is dulled a bit with all the mockery of Farquaad's height). "Fairytale" is a generationally good piece, wasted on this film.

Speed Racer

★★★★★ | A driving prodigy in a strange world battles global racing corruption.

An insane technical display of labyrinthian color, light, and sound that culminates into one of the most euphoric finales I've ever seen in a film. Devestatingly uplifting message about fighting the good fight against the corruption of beautiful things. This film is what happens when everyone capable of locking in locks in to make a movie about locking in.

Stand by Me

★★★★☆ | Four kids go on a journey to see a body.

Lifted by a lot of melancholy adolescent feelings. The main cast do an admirable job of forging a believable bond between the protagonists through the simple, honest lens of childhood.

Stone Cold

★★⯪☆☆ | A biker gang fights the Mississippi government.

Violent, explosive, and rather dumb. Heavy on expensive-looking stunts, but not grounded with much depth of storytelling.

Survivor: Pearl Islands (S7)

★★★⯪☆ | Sixteen contestants fight for one million dollars.

Solid television with a pretty well-rounded cast. The simpler, more straightforward version of Survivor is refreshing. A great reminder of how important theming is for framing player mindsets.

The Taking of Pelham 123

★★★★☆ | A tense hostage situation gets tangled in NYC bureaucracy.

Everything is ugly, urban, and miserable in this movie, it's such a vibe. Maybe the most New York City movie ever made. The final scene is nigh-perfect.

The Three Stooges (2012)

★☆☆☆☆ | The Stooges venture into the 21st century to save an orphanage.

A concept probably doomed to fail from the start. Excellent effort by the leads, but way too much plot for its own good and a supporting cast that mostly doesn't bring the right rhythm for this style of gag comedy. Like watching a plane fall out of the sky, unfortunately.

The Tower of the Elephant

★★★⯪☆ | Conan the Cimmerian climbs a tower to steal a magical gem.

I've seen pulp homaged and parodied so much that it's a shock to see a genuine article. Lines like "[This cord] was woven from the tresses of dead women, which I took from their tombs at midnight, and steeped in the deadly wine of the upas tree to give it strength," made me cackle in delight. We're too damned afraid of being cringe nowadays! I really wish we all collectively weren't so afraid of toeing the line into corny stuff. Massively entertaining with a twist like a bucket of cold water being thrown at you.

The Treasure of the Sierra Madre (1948)

★★★★★ | Paranoia ensues as greed's shadow casts over a desparate mining expedition.

This movie's texture is outstanding - sweaty, exhausted, miserable, with everyone folding themselves into knots over the prisoner's dilemma. It all resonates so clearly. Absolute home run of a movie.

Twisted Metal (Season 1)

★★⯪☆☆ | Post-apocalyptic road adventures with a surprising lack of twisting or metal.

Some very weird creative choices going on here. Anthony Mackie and Stephanie Beatriz do a wonderful job. Weird, absurd, great chemistry for the leads. A watchable, stupid show that passes the Icebox Test (but not much beyond that).

Wicked: For Good

★★☆☆☆ | The second act of Wicked.

Nope. Plenty of splendor and spectacle, but this does not address the writing issues of the original play (no resolution for Boc, the world twisting to force the characters into their Wizard of Oz versions, no justification for Morrible's surrender, no real plan for Elphaba's revolution, no in-film explanation for the drought, etc.). This film was a real opportunity to rewrite the second act of the musical into something stronger and they did not take it. Very nakedly not a well-built story.

The Wrecking Crew

★★★☆☆ | A documentary shining light on the most under-credited band in history.

A good documentary. Always interesting to peel back history and find hidden layers of things. Recognition long overdue. Didn't teach me much I didn't already know, but that's hardly the film's fault.
