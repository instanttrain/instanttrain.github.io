Review: Bic Cristal Xtra Smooth

September 30, 2026

I was a little baffled when I started seeing posts in writing and pen communities about the Bic Cristal - specifically, the "Xtra Smooth" and "Xtra Bold" variants. I used Cristals here and there growing up, but I switched to heavier, darker pens as I got older. I favored the Uniball 207 Signo Micro in high school for its sharp, pointed feel, and the Pilot G2 in college for its generous ink flow.

I remembered the Cristal as light and dull compared to more "serious" pens. What was all the fuss?

In Appreciation of Good Design

In the effort to sit down and do this review proper, I tried to sit down and take the Cristal seriously - to set aside any sort of "dollar store" notions and really study the product. In doing so, I realized the Cristal is the sort of modern miracle I tend to take for granted. The fact that a pen can be manufactered this cheap and still maintain its quality standard probably does have a hand in raising global literacy rates, as I've heard people claim. It's light, easy to distribute, universal, and affordable. Pretty close to a slam dunk as it gets for a good product, as far as I can tell.

(source: corporate.bic.com)

I admit that this is a strange comparison, but the way the translucent barrel provides an easy way to view the pen's ink reminds me of the health meter on Isaac's spine in Dead Space. It's the sort of information conveyance that's thoughtful about where the user can quickly look without disrupting the experience.

The barrel is six-sided to mimic the hand-feel of a pencil, there's enough ink that you usually lose these things before you empty them, the list goes on and on. Smart choices all around.

Just How Xtra?

I got a box of 10 pens for $1.57, so about 16 cents per pen. The pens inside had dust and fingerprints on them, but a lot of stores repurpose returns as new inventory nowadays. C'est la vie.

Compared to a standard Cristal (on the bottom), the Xtra Smooth (top) seems to be exactly the same, except for the "35% smoother ink" the box claims. Analysis time!

- Flow

The act of writing with the Xtra Smooth is... pretty marvelously smooth, yeah. The pen often takes a "startup" stroke to get the ink rolling, but once it does, it has a very reliable flow to it. The test passage above was written without any skips or bubbles in the lettering.

- Line Thickness / Pressure

The pen doesn't require much activation pressure (which is good, no marks being pressed into the paper), but as a result, the lines end up a bit light and thin. Purposefully rolling the pen to get a bad angle was solid as well; the pen writes at a tilt from 0° - 45° reliably. Dots, dashes, and shading all work just fine (it's a surprisingly good shading pen? Might try some manga-style halftones with this thing later.)

- Ink

Neither the box nor the pens specify the nib thickness, but I'm pretty sure these are Medium pens, specifically. The ink isn't as deep-black as Sharpie or Pilot inks, tending to land on more of a chestnut-black. Very readable.

- Emotion

Unfortunately, this is where the Xtra Smooth falls flat. The act of using the pen is a bit dull - pleasant enough and a reliable machine, but not a very passionate experience. The act of controlling a fountain pen, by comparison, has more interest: adding or relieving pressure to control the flow of ink, practicing and getting just the right scripting curve on the swoop of a P, or whatever. It's predictable, but a bit of a one-note experience (admittedly, it's trying to do that on purpose, and does it pretty marvelously).

Conclusion

There's a good argument against pretty much any complaint you could have about a Cristal - it's not a particularly exciting item, but it's built for reliability, not thrills. It could have a bit more weight or heft in its materials to give it a more luxurious feeling, but plastic keeps it easy to hold at all ages or hand sizes, as well as cheap to manufacture.

It's a darn good Bic. There's basically no reason to not have a few of these at your desk, and there's now a few at mine. I can't say it'll end up as my daily driver, but it serves as a great reminder of how sometimes a lack of a feature ceiling can sometimes mean a staggeringly high practicle floor.
